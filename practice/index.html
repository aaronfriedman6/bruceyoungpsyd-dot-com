How I work

My orientation is primarily psychodynamic. Psychodynamic treatment can take many forms, but at its core it aims to help people broaden their awareness and deepen their curiosity about what may seem totally trivial, blank, fixed, or too fleeting or murky to constructively think about. Learning about the unknown can be an anxiety-inducing but invigorating experience, and it often illuminates the shadowy mechanics of the problems that bring people to therapy. I try to take an active, authentic, and collaborative role in facilitating this exploration. Collaborative inquiry into how things are going in therapy provides an important vehicle for learning about what’s holding you back and how you can move forward in your broader life.

I sometimes draw on strategies from CBT, ACT, and DBT when initial assessment or subsequent exploration turns up issues that more concrete and targeted intervention may help with. Some clinical issues call for specialized experience I do not have, and at the end of our initial consultation sessions I may refer you to a better equipped clinician. I’ve often found, however, that a psychodynamic approach to exploration and collaboration can provide a helpful foundation for learning new skills, particularly in cases where problems are complex and chronic or when patients have previously struggled to benefit from more narrowly focused skills-based approaches.

I do not prescribe medication, but I sometimes collaborate with psychopharmacologists when a combination of therapy and medication seems like the ideal course of treatment. Depending on your presentation, I may recommend that you consult with a prescriber to consider the possibility of medication.
